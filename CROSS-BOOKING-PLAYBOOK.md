# Home Services Retention Department
## Cross-Booking Sales Playbook

**Purpose:** Help agents turn relevant customer conversations into additional, customer-approved home-service appointments.

**Departments:** Appliance Repair | HVAC | Electrical | Air Duct Cleaning | House Cleaning  
**Customer profile:** Homeowners  
**Call types:** Inbound and outbound  
**Market:** United States

> **Operating principle:** Help first. Recommend second. Book third. Never pressure a customer or recommend a service they do not need.

---

## 1. Assumptions and items management must approve

This playbook assumes:

- All five departments are operated by the same company.
- Agents may quote only approved prices or schedule an estimate.
- Promotions, discounts, financing, and bundled pricing require management approval.
- Appointments are subject to service-area coverage and real-time availability.
- Customers may decline additional services without affecting their original request.

Before launch, management must provide:

1. Approved service descriptions, prices, and estimate rules.
2. Discount authority and promotion terms.
3. Service-area coverage and scheduling capacity by department.
4. Cancellation, rescheduling, refund, and follow-up policies.
5. Safety escalation procedures.
6. CRM fields and call dispositions.

Do not invent prices, guarantees, availability, credentials, or service claims.

---

## 2. The five-step call process

1. **Connect:** Establish rapport and handle the reason for the call.
2. **Discover:** Ask a small number of relevant questions.
3. **Recommend:** Suggest one additional service only when the customer has a relevant need.
4. **Cross-book:** Check actual availability and explain approved pricing.
5. **Confirm:** Repeat the service, date, time window, price or estimate terms, and next steps.

**Agent rule:** Do not pitch all five departments on every call. The goal is a relevant recommendation, not the maximum number of offers.

---

## 3. Master inbound call script

### Opening and original request

“Thank you for calling [Company Name]. My name is [Agent Name]. How can I help you today?”

[Listen and handle the customer's original request.]

“Absolutely. I can help you get that taken care of.”

### Transition to a relevant additional service

“While I have you here, let me ask you one quick question. We also help homeowners with appliance repair, heating and cooling, electrical, air duct cleaning, and house cleaning.”

“Have you had any other issues around the home that you've been meaning to take care of?”

[Listen. Do not interrupt.]

### If the customer identifies a need

“Got it. We may be able to help with that as well. I can check whether that service is available in your area and explain the appointment options.”

“Would you like me to check availability?”

### If the customer declines

“No problem at all. I just wanted to make sure you knew we could help with those services too. Let's get your original request taken care of.”

### Confirmation

“Let me confirm: you're scheduled for [original service] on [date/time window]. You're also scheduled for [additional service] on [date/time window]. The approved price or estimate terms are [terms]. Is that all correct?”

“Thank you for choosing [Company Name]. We appreciate your business.”

---

## 4. Master outbound call script

### Opening

“Hi, may I speak with [Customer Name]?”

“Hi [Name], this is [Agent Name] calling from [Company Name]. We’ve helped you with a home service previously, and I'm reaching out to see whether there's anything else around the home you need help with. Is now a good time for a quick question?”

### Discover

“Since we last helped you, have you needed any appliance repair, HVAC service, electrical work, air duct cleaning, or house cleaning?”

“Is there anything you've noticed or been meaning to schedule?”

[Listen and identify one relevant need.]

### Recommend

“Based on what you've mentioned, [service] may be worth looking into. We can check whether it's available in your area and explain the scope and pricing before you decide.”

“Would you like me to check appointment options?”

### Close

“I can check [confirmed option A] or [confirmed option B]. Would either work for you?”

Only offer appointment times confirmed in the scheduling system.

### If not interested

“Of course. I don't want to recommend something you don't need. Would you prefer that we not follow up about additional services, or would a follow-up at another time be helpful?”

Record the customer's preference and follow company contact-permission rules.

## 5. Service-specific cross-booking scripts

Use these as conversation prompts, not automatic pairings. Ask about the customer's experience before recommending another department.

### A. Appliance repair

**When appliance repair is the original service**

“While we're arranging your appliance service, have your heating and cooling systems been working properly? If you've noticed uneven temperatures, unusual noises, or performance issues, we can also check availability for an HVAC appointment.”

**When appliance repair is the additional service**

“Have you noticed any issues with your refrigerator, oven, dishwasher, washer, or dryer? If something isn't working as expected, we can help arrange an appliance repair appointment.”

**Discovery prompts**
- Is any appliance not working as expected?
- Is there an issue you've been putting off?
- Which appliance is giving you the most trouble?

### B. HVAC

**When HVAC is the original service**

“While we're arranging your heating and cooling service, have you had your air ducts professionally cleaned before? If you're interested, I can explain what our duct cleaning service includes and check availability.”

**When HVAC is the additional service**

“How has your heating and cooling been working lately? Have you noticed uneven temperatures, unusual noises, or trouble keeping your home comfortable? If so, we can check availability for an HVAC appointment.”

**Discovery prompts**
- Is your home staying comfortable?
- Have you noticed unusual noises or changes in performance?
- When was your system last serviced?

### C. Electrical

**When electrical is the original service**

“While we're helping with that, have you noticed any other electrical concerns around the home, such as outlets that aren't working, lights flickering, or breakers tripping? If so, we can help arrange an appointment to have those concerns evaluated.”

**When electrical is the additional service**

“Have you noticed any electrical issues around your home recently? If there's something you've been meaning to have checked, we can help coordinate an appointment.”

**Discovery prompts**
- Are all your outlets and lights working normally?
- Have you noticed breakers tripping?
- Is there an electrical concern you want a qualified professional to evaluate?

**Safety escalation:** If a customer reports smoke, sparks, a burning smell, exposed live wiring, or another immediate electrical danger, stop cross-selling and follow the company's emergency safety procedure. Do not diagnose the issue over the phone or minimize the danger.

### D. Air duct cleaning

**When air duct cleaning is the original service**

“While we're arranging your duct cleaning appointment, have you had your HVAC system checked recently? We can also help coordinate HVAC service if you want to look into that.”

**When air duct cleaning is the additional service**

“Have you had your air ducts professionally cleaned before, or is that something you've been considering? I can explain what our service includes and check availability if you'd like.”

**Discovery prompts**
- When was the last professional duct cleaning?
- Are you interested in learning what the service includes?
- Would you like to review the scope before deciding?

Do not claim that duct cleaning is necessary based only on an HVAC issue, allergies, dust, or other symptoms. Do not make unsupported health or indoor-air-quality claims.

### E. House cleaning

**When house cleaning is the original service**

“While we're arranging your cleaning, would you prefer a one-time service or are you interested in recurring cleaning?”

**When house cleaning is the additional service**

“We also offer professional house cleaning. Would having help with the cleaning be useful right now? If so, would you be interested in a one-time cleaning or a recurring schedule?”

**Discovery prompts**
- Do you currently use a professional cleaning service?
- Would a one-time or recurring cleaning be more useful?
- What areas or tasks would you want included?

Never promise a cleaning scope that has not been confirmed.

| Electrical | Appliance repair, HVAC |

The agent must establish a genuine customer need before recommending the additional service.

## 7. Offer concepts for management to test

These are offer structures, not approved promotions. Management must validate pricing, capacity, scope, and gross margin before agents use them.

### Offer 1: Multi-service convenience

“Since you're already scheduling with us, I can check whether we can coordinate both services in a convenient appointment window.”

### Offer 2: Seasonal home maintenance

“We're helping homeowners plan ahead for the season. Would you like us to check availability for [relevant service]?”

### Offer 3: Returning-customer promotion

“As a returning customer, you may qualify for one of our current promotions. Let me check which offers are available for that service.”

### Offer 4: Two-service bundle

“I can quote the services separately, or check whether there's an approved combined-service price. Would you like me to compare those options?”

Only quote a combined price after verifying the approved price and margin.

### Offer 5: Recurring house cleaning

“If the first cleaning works well for you, we can also discuss a recurring schedule. Would weekly, every two weeks, or monthly be more convenient?”

Do not imply a discount, priority, free service, or special eligibility unless the CRM or approved offer sheet confirms it.

## 8. Objection handling

### “I'm not interested.”

“I completely understand. I don't want to recommend something you don't need. Let's focus on the service you originally called about.”

### “I already have a company.”

“That's great. If you're happy with your current provider, I wouldn't want you to change something that's working. If you ever need another option, we're happy to help.”

### “It's too expensive.”

“I understand. Nobody wants to spend money unnecessarily. I can explain exactly what's included and confirm the approved price so you can decide whether it makes sense.”

### “I need to think about it.”

“Of course. Is there any information about the service, scope, or price that would help you make a decision?”

“Would you like us to follow up, or would you prefer to contact us when you're ready?”

### “I don't have time.”

“I understand. Would another day or a different appointment window be more convenient? I can check the available options.”

### “Can you give me a discount?”

“I can check whether there's an approved promotion for that service. Let me verify what's currently available rather than promise you something I can't authorize.”

### “Just take care of the original service.”

“Absolutely. That's our priority. I'll get your original request taken care of.”

**Never argue, guilt the customer, manufacture urgency, or continue after a clear refusal.**

---

## 9. Two-option close

Use this only after the customer has expressed interest.

- “Would you prefer to schedule that separately, or should I check whether we can coordinate it with your existing appointment?”
- “Would [confirmed option A] or [confirmed option B] work better?”
- “Would you like a one-time cleaning, or would a recurring schedule be more useful?”

Do not present false choices. The customer can always decline.

## 10. Customer discovery prompts

Agents should ask two or three relevant questions, not interrogate the customer.

**General home maintenance**
- Is everything around the home working the way it should?
- Is there anything you've been meaning to have checked?
- Are there any home projects you've been putting off?

**Appliances**
- Is every major appliance working properly?
- Have you noticed anything unusual with an appliance?

**HVAC**
- Is your home staying comfortable?
- Have you noticed changes in heating or cooling performance?

**Electrical**
- Are your outlets and lights working normally?
- Have you noticed breakers tripping or other electrical concerns?

**Air ducts**
- Have you had your ductwork professionally cleaned before?
- Would you like to learn what our duct cleaning service includes?

**House cleaning**
- Do you currently use a professional cleaning service?
- Would a one-time or recurring cleaning be more useful?

## 11. CRM fields and call dispositions

Capture the following for every eligible call:

- Customer name and account ID
- Call type: inbound or outbound
- Original department
- Additional department discussed
- Customer-stated need
- Service-area eligibility
- Appointment availability checked
- Offer or price quoted
- Additional appointment booked: yes/no
- Appointment date and time window
- Booking value
- Follow-up requested: yes/no
- Customer declined: yes/no
- Do-not-contact or contact preference, as applicable
- Cancellation status
- Job completion status
- Completed revenue and gross profit, when available

Suggested dispositions:
- Original service only
- Cross-sell offered — customer interested
- Cross-sell booked
- Cross-sell follow-up requested
- Cross-sell declined
- Not eligible — outside service area
- Not eligible — no capacity
- Safety escalation
- Customer requested no additional-service calls

## 12. Retention department scorecard

| KPI | Formula |
|---|---|
| Cross-book rate | Additional bookings ÷ eligible calls |
| Offer rate | Calls with a relevant offer ÷ eligible calls |
| Offer conversion | Additional bookings ÷ relevant offers |
| Revenue per eligible call | Additional booked revenue ÷ eligible calls |
| Average additional ticket | Additional booked revenue ÷ additional bookings |
| Show/completion rate | Completed additional jobs ÷ additional bookings |
| Cancellation rate | Cancelled additional bookings ÷ additional bookings |
| Gross profit per eligible call | Additional gross profit ÷ eligible calls |

### Illustrative monthly example

| Metric | Example |
|---|---:|
| Eligible calls | 2,000 |
| Cross-book rate | 15% |
| Additional appointments booked | 300 |
| Average additional ticket | $250 |
| Additional booked revenue | $75,000 |
| Revenue per eligible call | $37.50 |

These figures are illustrative targets for demonstrating the math, not a forecast. Booked revenue is not gross profit. Track completed jobs, refunds, cancellations, technician costs, and gross profit.

### Daily agent tracking

- Calls handled
- Eligible cross-sell opportunities
- Relevant additional services offered
- Additional appointments booked
- Booked revenue
- Customer declines
- Follow-up requests
- Cancellations
- Completed jobs, when available

## 13. Coaching and quality assurance

Review a sample of calls weekly. Score each item:

| Quality item | Score |
|---|---:|
| Handled original request correctly | 0 or 1 |
| Asked relevant discovery question(s) | 0 or 1 |
| Identified a genuine customer need | 0 or 1 |
| Recommended a relevant service | 0 or 1 |
| Explained scope and approved pricing accurately | 0 or 1 |
| Asked permission to check availability | 0 or 1 |
| Confirmed booking accurately | 0 or 1 |
| Respected refusal and contact preferences | 0 or 1 |

**Maximum score:** 8

Coach the behavior that is missing. Do not tell agents to increase offers indiscriminately. Improve relevance, clarity, and booking execution.

---

## 14. Value Equation: practical application

Use this as an internal coaching lens:

- **Dream outcome:** A well-maintained, comfortable home.
- **Likelihood of achievement:** Qualified service professionals, clear service scope, and reliable appointment processes.
- **Time delay:** Convenient scheduling and coordinated appointments when available.
- **Effort and sacrifice:** One company and one conversation can reduce the customer's effort of finding and coordinating multiple providers.

Customer-facing message:

“We make it easier to take care of your home by helping coordinate the services you actually need.”

Do not promise outcomes that the company cannot substantiate.

---

## 15. Launch checklist

Before rollout:

1. Approve service descriptions and pricing.
2. Establish promotion terms and discount limits.
3. Confirm service areas and appointment capacity by department.
4. Publish safety escalation procedures.
5. Train agents on the five service categories.
6. Add cross-booking fields and dispositions to the CRM.
7. Train agents on inbound and outbound scripts.
8. Review calls and customer feedback.
9. Track completed jobs, cancellations, and gross profit—not just bookings.
10. Review results weekly and improve the weakest part of the process.

## Final operating rule

**Help first. Recommend second. Book third.**

A customer who trusts your company is more valuable than a customer who feels pressured into buying five services.
